Running a Player Auction on Paper and WhatsApp, and How a Live Auction Fixes It

Most local cricket and football leagues still run their player auction the old way: a printed list of players, a whiteboard for the purses, bids shouted across a hall, and a WhatsApp group where someone posts the results — hours later, if at all. It works, until the tournament grows. Then the arithmetic slips, two owners claim the same bid, and the auction meant to finish by 9 PM drags past midnight. A live auction platform fixes this by moving the whole event onto one shared screen: every bid is recorded the moment it is made, every purse updates itself, and every team sees the same numbers. This is how PowerAuction does it.

The paper and WhatsApp auction

Picture auction night in a typical local league. The organiser has a spreadsheet of 100 or more players, printed in order. Owners bid by voice or paddle, a helper writes the winning bid on the whiteboard and subtracts it from the team's purse on a calculator, and another helper updates a second spreadsheet for the projector. Meanwhile, a volunteer posts sold players into a WhatsApp group for the owners who could not come.

For a small auction — six teams, forty players — this works. Add more teams, more players and more money, and it starts to creak: the whiteboard and the spreadsheet disagree, the WhatsApp group is three players behind, and the auctioneer cannot say whether a team can still bid without stopping to check.

Where the manual auction breaks

First, the purse. Every team starts with a fixed budget, and every purchase has to come out of it. On paper, that subtraction happens by hand after every sale, and one wrong figure can trigger a dispute mid-auction. Worse, a team can spend money it does not have: without a live check, an owner can win a player at a price that leaves too little to fill the rest of the squad.

Second, the bid itself. When bids are called out loud, two owners often claim the same bid — and one walks away feeling cheated, with no record to check. Third, speed: every pause to verify a purse adds a minute, and across 150 players those minutes become hours. The hall empties, and the last players get rushed through at midnight. When it ends, the final squads and sold prices live in scattered notes, a spreadsheet on one laptop and a WhatsApp group nobody can search.

Live bidding: how it works on PowerAuction

PowerAuction replaces the whiteboard, the calculator and the WhatsApp group with one system everyone watches at once. The organiser sets it up first: the teams, each with a purse and squad rules; the players, registered with a photo, role, category and base price; and the bidding rules — starting price, bid steps and timer.

On auction night, the auctioneer runs it all from one console. Each player comes up with their card on the big screen: photo, role, base price and category. Owners bid from their own phones, from the hall or from anywhere else. The moment a bid is placed, it is checked and pushed to every screen instantly. The price, the leading team and the timer are the same everywhere, so there is nothing to argue about. Every bid restarts the timer, so a player sells when the bidding genuinely stops, and a 150-player auction finishes on schedule.

Purses that enforce themselves

The biggest change is what happens to the purse. In PowerAuction, the purse is not a number someone updates — it is a rule the system enforces. After every sale, the winning bid is deducted automatically, and every screen shows the team's remaining purse and the squad slots still to fill.

Before a bid is accepted, the server checks it: is the lot still open, does the bid beat the current price by at least the increment, and can the team afford it while keeping enough to fill the remaining slots at base price? A bid that would break the rules is rejected before it appears on any screen, with the reason shown. The team cannot overspend by accident, and the organiser never stops the auction for the maths. Owners see the same arithmetic — their own purse and the most they can bid — plus every rival's purse, so the bidding stays strategic instead of blind.

The big screen and the room

An auction is a show, and the hall should feel like one. PowerAuction puts the auction on a projector or LED screen: the player's card, the current price, the timer, every team's purse and the sponsors. Players, supporters and sponsors can follow every bid, and anyone with the link can watch live on their own phone too. The auctioneer keeps full control from the console — pause, undo a wrong bid or result, adjust a purse if the league decides to — and because the console is the only place that can change the auction's state, two devices can never disagree about whether a lot is still open. Teams that prefer paddles can still take part: the auctioneer enters their bids from the console, with the same checks applying.

After the hammer falls

When the last player is sold, the work that used to take a week is already done. Every squad is final, every sold price is recorded, every unsold player is listed — and the organiser can export it all to a spreadsheet or share the results immediately. No rebuilding from notes or retyping from the whiteboard. Unsold players can come up again in a second round or go into a draft, and the league keeps a clean record it can reuse next season.

Frequently asked questions

How many players can a live auction handle?

PowerAuction is built for a real local league — 100 to 200 players across 8 to 12 teams is a normal night, and bids, purses and squads update automatically, so the auction moves at the pace of the bidding.

Can team owners bid if they cannot come to the hall?

Yes. Owners bid from their phones, from the hall or from anywhere else, and see the same price, timer and purses as everyone in the room. Teams that prefer paddles can still take part — the auctioneer enters their bids.

Can a team accidentally spend more than its purse?

No. Every bid is checked against the team's remaining purse and squad rules before it is accepted, and a bid that would leave the team unable to fill its squad is rejected — so overspending is impossible.

What do we get after the auction ends?

Final squads for every team, every sold price, the unsold players, and a complete bid history — ready to export to a spreadsheet and share, or show straight on the big screen and the live link.
